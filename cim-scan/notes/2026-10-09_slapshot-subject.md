- **What it is:** K-12 courseware and virtual-school platform selling to districts -
  $26.9M ARR at June 2026 (up from $7.8M in 2024), forecast CARR $39.6M FY26E to
  $605.9M FY31P and revenue $27.3M to $474.9M; Adj. EBITDA ($12.3M) in FY26E, turning
  positive in FY29P and reaching $194.8M (41% margin) in FY31P; ~80% gross margin.
- **What they did:** Made the AI the revenue line itself. The ARR table is a list of AI
  SKUs: Courses AI $16.2M (60.2% of ARR), Teacher of Record AI $4.9M (18.2%, from zero in
  FY2024), Credit Recovery and Multilingual AI $5.8M together. AI SKUs are priced per
  student: Courses AI $500, Multilingual AI $700, Teacher of Record AI $2,500-6,000.
  The operating model is AI-produced: "~85% of content AI-generated," state-certified
  teachers on 1099 with an AI assistant "letting one teacher support 100+ students,"
  an agentic grading pipeline (Researcher, Grader, Summarizer, human-in-the-loop) running
  ~4.9M AI grading events per month, and 60B+ AI tokens used in Q2 2026.
- **Evidence:** In the numbers on both sides of the P&L - AI SKUs are the ARR, and AI is
  the cost structure. Engineering is explicit: "92% of new code written by AI in Q1
  2026," features ship 2-3x faster, "comparable output with a leaner team (7 engineers
  today vs. 15 at the 2024-25 peak)" - a headcount reduction attributed to AI. Vendor
  posture is model-agnostic ("best-model-for-task routing across all frontier vendors"
  with automated shadow A/B testing) rather than a named provider. Outcome evidence is
  a district case study (Coachella Valley: Grade 11 ELA proficiency 36.5% to 40.8% in
  the first full year).
- **Read:** The first CIM in the corpus where AI is the product rather than a lever on
  an existing one - the question shifts from whether the AI is real to whether a ~17x
  revenue ramp to FY31P is.
- **Relevant portcos:**
  - Career Certified - because it is an online accredited-education business, and
    Subject is the AI-native version of that model: ~85% AI-generated course content
    at ~80% gross margin. Houlihan Lokey's AI Readiness Report for Career Certified
    (Jan 2026, in the Vault) shows bankers already scoring it on exactly this axis.
  - HSI - because its training and compliance content library (Helix materials, Mar
    2026, in the Vault) is produced the way Subject's incumbents produce theirs, and
    Subject's pitch is precisely that legacy text-based libraries are "structurally
    disadvantaged."
  - Talogy - because assessment is its core IP, and ~4.9M AI grading events per month
    is automated assessment at a scale that resets buyer expectations for unit cost.
- **Question:** Subject produces ~85% of its course content with AI. What share of
  Career Certified's and HSI's content production is still human-authored today - and
  if a buyer benchmarks either against an AI-native producer, does the content library
  still read as a moat or as a cost base?
