- **What it is:** Mortgage credit-score optimization SaaS - $25M ARR Apr-26, 109%
  NRR, 99% gross retention, 79% of ARR enterprise, converted from transactional to
  contracted SaaS (base minimums plus usage overages) through 2024-25.
- **What they did:** Layered an agentic future on an ML-native core: an "Agentic AI
  Layer - Autonomous Orchestration & Decision Intelligence" drawn above the API layer
  and explicitly marked "Represents Future Use Cases," and a 3Q26-2Q28 roadmap of
  agentic core functionalities, multi-LLM orchestration, model intelligence upgrades,
  and borrower-engagement AI.
- **Evidence:** Current economics are attributed entirely to the SaaS conversion and
  usage-driven expansion ($17.3M base / $20.0M total ARR at 2025 exit, $2.4M upsell
  ARR in Apr-26 LTM) - no AI attribution in the growth story. The predictive
  score-optimization engine is the legacy core IP, but the generative/agentic layer
  is roadmap-only with an honest "future use cases" label. Tier 2.
- **Read:** A seller separating what is priced today from what is drawn on the
  architecture slide. The multi-LLM orchestration line is becoming standard roadmap
  furniture in software CIMs this cohort.
- **Relevant portcos:**
  - PracticeTek - because CreditXpert's usage-based monetization of analytics
    modules on top of a contracted SaaS base is the same upsell math PracticeTek's
    payments and module attach runs on; the roadmap shows how an AI module gets
    staged before it gets priced.
- **Question:** CreditXpert labels its agentic layer "future use cases" while
  charging for what works today. When WCP portcos present AI roadmaps to boards or
  buyers, do we enforce the same separation between priced-today and
  future-use-case - and would each portco's current deck survive that test?
