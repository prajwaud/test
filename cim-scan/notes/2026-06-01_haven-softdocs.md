- **What they did:** Put AI-embedded productivity into the margin walk as a named driver
  alongside AWS migration, platform build-out and GTM efficiency, flowing to ~30%
  billings-based adjusted EBITDA margin expansion 2023A to 2031P. Cost stack mapped by
  line: COGS 25.6% to 18.6% of billings-based revenue, expensed R&D 12.7% to 4.8%,
  AI impact assigned to R&D, S&M and G&A ("cross-bucket efficiency lift"). Separately
  quantified internal gains: ~50% reduction in handling time from automated
  classification and extraction, ~60% faster transcript review, 3x RFP response volume
  with no additional staff, 20% faster MQL conversion, ~$300K OpEx reduction in
  content/consulting plus ~$250K hosting savings, with support, services and GTM
  scaling "without proportional headcount." AI workloads run Bedrock-native on AWS.
- **Evidence:** Strongest in the June cohort. AI sits as a bridge driver a buyer can
  price, and the internal gains are task-level and number-carrying (sourced to a
  third-party tech report and management). Two gaps: AI is not sized separately from
  the other three margin levers, and the headcount claim is avoidance going forward,
  not a realized reduction.
- **Read:** This is the presentation standard for a software exit now - AI as a named
  line next to boring levers, not an AI slide. The avoided-headcount framing ("scale
  without proportional headcount") is the polite version of the cost story and reads
  as more credible than a cut.
