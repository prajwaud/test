- **What it is:** Salesforce-native post-sale project execution SaaS (customer
  onboarding and delivery workflows) - ~$15M ARR growing 17% YoY, ~95% recurring,
  ~15% EBITDA margins, ~420 customers, 110% net revenue retention (116% net dollar in
  top-quartile ICP accounts), Financial Services its largest vertical at 36% of
  revenue.
- **What they did:** Put AI revenue in the forecast and priced it three ways. The
  P&L build carries an AI-related recurring revenue line "expected to grow from
  approximately $53k in FY27E (~80% contracted) to $450k in FY28E." The engagement
  model page prices the stack: MCP access tied to seats, "proprietary agent licensing
  stands up as the premium line," and an "outcome-based pricing pilot - pay per
  measured improvement." Delivery is dated: proprietary agent pack v1 in production
  internally by end of July 2026, first customer-facing pack in Q4, a TaskRay-hosted
  MCP server shipping June 2026 with RBAC and rate limiting. One named customer
  carries "$240k+ of contracted upsell including TaskRay's new AI offerings."
  Leadership is pitched as an "AI-first operating model" with an "agent-ready product
  strategy."
- **Evidence:** The AI line is in the numbers and mostly contracted - Tier 4 - and it
  is conspicuously small ($53k on $15M ARR), which reads as underwriting discipline
  rather than a thesis crutch. Integration targets are named vendors: Claude, ChatGPT,
  Gemini, AWS Bedrock (model_provider_named). The defensive positioning is explicit
  too: "In an Agentic World, the Record Keeps its Value" and "Agentforce complements
  rather than replaces TaskRay" - a seller arguing its system of record survives agent
  disruption.
- **Read:** The most complete AI monetization architecture in the corpus so far:
  contracted revenue, three pricing models, dated agent shipments, and a
  survive-the-agents defense - all sized honestly. This is what the mature version of
  the Marketron claim looks like when a seller expects diligence to check.
- **Relevant portcos:**
  - PracticeTek - because it monetizes module and payments attach on a vertical SaaS
    base, and TaskRay's pricing architecture (seat-tied MCP access, licensed agents as
    the premium line, an outcome-based pilot) is the concrete menu of choices for
    pricing PracticeTek's own AI modules.
  - TeamSnap and Science Exchange - because TaskRay is the fourth CIM in this corpus
    with MCP on the roadmap (after Optimy, CreditXpert and New Reach), which makes
    agent interoperability standard seller furniture; the question of whether our
    platforms are MCP-addressable will come from buyers next.
- **Question:** TaskRay shows ~$53k of contracted AI revenue and calls it a line item;
  most sellers show zero and call it a strategy. Does any WCP software portco have
  even $50k of contracted AI revenue today - and if not, which of TaskRay's three
  pricing models (seat-tied access, licensed agents, outcome-based) is the right first
  test, at which portco?
